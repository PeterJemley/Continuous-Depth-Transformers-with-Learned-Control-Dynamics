Voices and Directions

A listener follows several voices in music at once, each a continuous line. The project asked whether a language model could hold several readings of a text the same way. These drawings set the idea beside what the tests measured, where things went wrong, and some connections that are imagined rather than shown.

The paper · replications, T1

At this budget, most of the gap came from a shortcut

Measured

The paper's model put a continuous block inside a transformer. Trained the paper's way, it trailed an ordinary transformer. At a tenth of the training budget, changing one difference at a time showed where the gap came from: about 95% of it was the memory-saving shortcut used to compute the block's learning signal. The paper's refuted claims stay refuted.

Chapter 1 · G1, G1c, G1d, N1, N2

Here, the flow did not carry the readings

Measured

If the continuous block carried the readings, its paths should bend about twice as differently under each reading (G1), and pull nearby states together about twice as fast along the farther reading's directions (N2). They differed only slightly, by 8 to 27% and by 14 to 25%. Over the whole state, the block did pull 4 to 10 times harder under the farther reading (G1c). But capping most of that pull cost the farther task no more than the nearer one, −0.002 nats on average (G1d): real, but not, by this test, needed. G1, G1c and G1d added a control signal into the flow; N2's model had none. All ran at a tenth of the training, and multiplicative control and longer training were not tested.

Chapters 2 and 3 · N4 to N9

The readings live in directions

Measured geometry

The model's internal state has 256 directions. We describe each reading by the 16 directions its loss is most sensitive to. The further ahead a reading looks, the less those directions overlap with the next-word reading's. Forcing two readings into the same directions cost 0.088 nats (N4).

The scrambled-answer head is the surprise. It reads the same word as the clean head, yet its directions overlap the clean head's nearly as little as a reading four words ahead does (0.31 against 0.25), far from the 0.95 of two heads reading the same word. Nor is it close to an ideal noisy copy of the right reading (N9b). Trained on noise, it reads differently.

Chapter 4 · N12 to N18

Directions roughly follow accuracy

Measured

Heads given fewer and fewer genuine answers form a ladder: the worse a head predicts, the less it reads the clean head's directions. Mixture heads, trained on scrambled and genuine answers together, sit close to that ladder (N14). So do most heads whose answers were weighted by a judge; every N18 head drawn here stayed within 0.03 of it, the self-weighting judge's far down. The clear exception is N14's head weighted by its own discount, 0.10 above: it reads the clean head's directions better than its accuracy predicts. So the ladder is not a law.

The error we kept chasing · N16 to N18

A judge that grades itself

Measured

The remedy for scrambled answers weights each one by a judge's estimate that it is genuine. When the clean head judges, the remaining shortfall vanishes (N16). Judges trained at full weight on fewer genuine answers form a line: the less accurate the judge, the less of the shortfall it recovers. Judges caught in a loop, weighting their own training by their own predictions, fall below that line. So does the loop-free twin, by less: d = −0.18, against −0.29 and −0.46.

Where the picture stands

A corner filled in, the centre still open

The pieces glued down so far say that, at this scale, two readings occupy partly different directions and forcing them together costs accuracy, while the flow's bend and per-reading pull did not differ as the conjecture needed; and that a head trained on scrambled answers is not a noisy copy of the right reading. Half-glued: on genuine answers alone, a judge that weights its own training does harm on its own (N18a), and an exploratory split puts most of that through its own loss of accuracy. None of them yet tests a model holding several readings the way a listener holds several voices. Most tests used one model size and a tenth of the full training, and the judge tests one noise rate, 80%.
